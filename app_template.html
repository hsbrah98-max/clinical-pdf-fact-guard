Snowpine Schools

Mental health screening for high schools, and a ready intake for the psychiatrist.

Schools run depression and anxiety screening and collect teacher ADHD ratings and IEP, 504, and testing records in one place, for free. Families who want care choose a child and adolescent psychiatrist, who receives the packet already organized.

Who pays
SchoolFree. Screening, teacher forms, records, and safety alerts.
FamilyFree. They see the result in plain words and decide whether to share it and with whom.
Psychiatry practiceA flat monthly listing in the family directory, plus the Snowpine suite (Letters, Superbills, Private model). No fee per referral, and the directory is not ranked by payment.
Why not sell leads per student
  • FERPA: a school cannot release a student's results without parent consent.
  • HIPAA: once a practice holds the data, selling it is prohibited.
  • State referral laws: paying per patient referred is barred in many states, including California under B&P §650.
  • Letting the family choose gets the same students to practices, in a form a district can approve.
  • The directory also lists county, community, and school-based options for free, so counselors can trust it.
Before a pilot school
  • Prior written parent consent for screening (PPRA). The counselor screen tracks it per student.
  • A named person at the school who answers same-day safety alerts, and a written protocol.
  • A BAA-covered model endpoint, and a FERPA data agreement with the district.
  • A license for the Vanderbilt teacher scale, or a switch to an open scale. PHQ-A and GAD-7 are free to use.
  • Healthcare counsel to review the listing model before any practice pays.

What counselors ask first

A student answers yes to the suicide question at 3:45 on a Friday. Then what?

The check-in only opens during the school day and closes at 2:30 pm, so answers arrive while staff are on site. A yes alerts you and your named backup at once. You run the brief ASQ suicide risk screen (free from NIMH), meet the student, and call the parent under your district's protocol. If an alert is still open at the end of the day, it goes to your district's on-call contact. Snowpine never replaces your protocol. It makes sure nobody misses an alert.

Is this a way to sell my students to private practices?

No. Families see the results first and decide. The directory lists county mental health, community clinics, and your own wellness center for free, sorted by distance and filtered by the family's insurance. Private practices pay a flat listing fee. No one pays per student, and no one can pay for a higher spot.

What does this add to my week?

Consent requests, reminders, scoring, and teacher follow-ups run on their own. What is left is the follow-up conversations you would want to have anyway, with a list of who needs you first. We will measure the actual hours in the pilot rather than promise a number now.

What about privacy?

Parents give written consent before any screening, as PPRA requires. Nothing reaches a practice unless a parent signs for it, item by item, and they can withdraw it later. The district signs a FERPA data agreement before the pilot starts.

Is screening at school actually recommended?

Yes. The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening adolescents ages 12 to 18 for depression and children and adolescents ages 8 to 18 for anxiety. USPSTF, JAMA, October 2022.

A pilot looks like one grade for one semester, at no cost to the school, with a check-in call with you every two weeks.

For child and adolescent psychiatrists

New adolescent patients who arrive with their records already read.

Families find you through their high school's screening program and choose to share their records with you. You get the check-in scores, teacher ADHD ratings, and a summary of the IEP, 504, and testing, with every fact tied to its page, before the first visit.

What you get
  • Referrals that fit: you set ages, conditions, insurance, and a weekly cap
  • The packet: scores, teacher ratings, records summary, and a list of what is still missing
  • The letter, half written: the IEP or 504 support letter drafts in Snowpine Letters, in your voice
  • Superbills from your paper day sheet, for out-of-network families
  • One flat monthly fee. No per-referral charge, ever.

What psychiatrists ask first

Will I be flooded with referrals I cannot take?

No. You set the ages you see, the conditions you treat, the insurance you take, and a weekly cap. When you are full, your listing says so, and families see the next option. Pausing takes one click.

If a packet shows a positive suicide screen, am I responsible before I have seen the patient?

The school completes a same-day safety check before results ever reach the family, and your packet shows that it was done. You can decline any referral, and the family is sent back to the directory right away. Confirm the details with your own counsel and malpractice carrier. We will put this in writing in the practice agreement.

Why not just pay per referral?

Paying per patient referred runs into state anti-referral and fee-splitting laws, such as California's B&P §650, and puts your license at risk. A flat listing keeps you clean, and families trust a directory that nobody can buy their way up.

What does it cost?

One flat monthly listing that includes Snowpine Letters and Superbills. We are setting the price with the first pilot practices.
